I N T R O D U C T I O N

Nowadays, the development of science and technology
can be accomplished by manufacturing more functional
materials. A systematic approach for improving the per-
formance of materials is to study their structure. In this
regard, obtaining information about probable imperfec-
tions in the structure and the underlying mechanisms
involved in their formation provides scientific and indus-
trial societies with the opportunity to modify the synthe-
sis and manufacturing processes. Although conventional
methods of materials characterization, for instance, labo-
ratory X-ray diffraction (XRD) and conventional emission
spectroscopy, are useful for this purpose, they have limita-
tions. One of the most effective solutions for dealing with
the constraints of conventional methods is the employ-
ment of SR and the development of its facilities. SR facili-
ties produce high-flux, monochromatic, highly collimated
X-ray beams with high brightness. The energy of the emit-
ted beams can be tuned in a manner that contributes to

obtaining signals with a high signal-to-noise ratio. As a
result of
the advantageous characteristics of SR-based
X-rays, it is possible to study crystalline, amorphous, and
complex structures elaborately. There is a wide range of
applications of SR, especially in materials characterization
for investigating properties of metals, ceramics, polymers,
nanomaterials, and biomaterials. Considering that
the
main objective of this study is to demonstrate the feasibil-
ity of SR for materials characterization, the remainder of
the paper is organized as follows. First, the characteristics
of SR were elucidated by presenting some features of new
generation SR facilities. Afterward, different characteriza-
tion techniques using X-ray beams, namely, X-ray powder
diffraction (XRPD), small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS),
X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS), techniques of imag-
ing, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and X-ray
microprobes/nanoprobes, are briefly explicated. Multiple
case studies regarding the implementation of aforemen-
tioned methods by laboratory X-rays and SR-emitted
X-rays are highlighted, clarifying the superiority of SR over

CHARACTERISTICS

Synchrotron radiation (SR) is electromagnetic radiation
emitted by accelerating electrons close to the speed of light.
A facility for producing SR X-rays consists of (a) an electron
accelerator (gun), to accelerate electrons to a given energy
(of the order of hundreds of MeV to multi-GeV); (b) a high
vacuum storage ring, into which the accelerated electrons in
the form of pulses (bunches) are injected; and (c) magnetic
field devices, such as bending magnets, wigglers, and
undulators, to tune the emitted light and conduct it to the
beamlines. Also, SR facilities enable the ability to select
wavelength and spot using devices such as slits, mirrors, and
monochromators. Figures 1 and 2 show a view of the Trieste
SR facility and a schematic of the fundamental components
of SR facilities, respectively.

undulators (which are insertion devices [IDs] in third-

and their distinguishing character is the difference in their
wavelength. Wigglers demonstrate spectra similar to the
ones acquired by bending magnets, whereas undulators
provide an intense pseudomonochromatic light.[2–4] Fur-

case of wigglers. Therefore, undulators can be considered

smaller areas in the samples. Characteristics of the most

3.1

| X-ray powder diffraction

Many materials are only available in polycrystalline form.
Thus, powder diffraction analysis can be used to study
the structure of these materials and determine their prop-
erties. In this regard, as a result of the high flux and bril-
liance of SR, one can obtain diffraction patterns with a
high signal-to-noise ratio and distinct peaks. Simulta-
neously, it is possible to study complex structures using
the Rietveld refinement method. The phase composition,
lattice parameters, residual strain, crystal structure, and
much more valuable information can be obtained using
this method. Furthermore, the combination of the maxi-
mum entropy method (MEM) with Rietveld refinement
for XRPD data can provide a high-resolution electron
density distribution. This method is especially useful for
investigating nanostructured materials.

RE FER EN CES

With respect to the daily development of advanced and
more complex materials for fulfilling the demands of
humanity, researchers require more effective tools to char-
acterize materials. Metals and alloys, advanced ceramics,
polymers, and composites, as the main engineering mate-
rials, are developing faster than ever, and their applica-
tions are increasing in different fields such as energy
issues, biomaterial, aerospace, automotive and transporta-
tion, marine issues, and other industries. Hence, because
the properties of materials are strongly related to their
structure, this requires studies providing detailed informa-
tion about the mechanisms and phenomena influencing
the structure and properties. The studies reviewed in this
paper conclude that SR with its high flux, monochromatic,
and low divergence radiation is a potent and invaluable
research tool for characterizing materials. Applying differ-
ent techniques, complementary data can be obtained con-
cerning the surface or depth of the materials. The SR-
based characterization techniques are very productive for
achieving precise information. Some of the advantages of
utilizing SR, proving superiority of SR X-ray beam over
laboratory ones, can be highlighted as follows:

1. The capability to study the occupancy of light atoms
in structures, as a result of SR high flux and high
signal-to-noise ratio of the obtained signals.

2. Calculation of microstresses within the grains of struc-

tures and through the depth of the samples.

3. The ability to conduct in situ experiments at high tem-
perature and in a multiphase system, besides quick
data acquisition in techniques requiring variety of
incident beam energy.
